04 BRAIN MRI KNOWLEDGE CENTER

Know the categories.

Learn what each label means, what is known about possible causes, and why an MRI label alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

EDUCATIONAL REFERENCE
01GLIAL CELLS

Glioma

A group of tumors that begin in glial cells or related precursor cells in the brain or central nervous system. Behaviour ranges from slower-growing to aggressive, depending on the specific type.

What causes it?

The exact cause of most gliomas is unknown. They develop alongside genetic changes in cells; rare inherited syndromes and certain exposures, such as ionising radiation, can increase risk for some brain tumors.

Why MRI matters

MRI helps clinicians examine the tumor's location, size, and appearance. Imaging findings alone do not reliably determine every tumor's type or grade.

02MENINGES

Meningioma

A tumor that arises from the meninges, the protective layers surrounding the brain and spinal cord. Many are slow-growing and non-cancerous, but some behave more aggressively.

What causes it?

Most cases have no single known cause. Risk is associated with factors such as increasing age, certain inherited conditions, and previous radiation exposure; hormonal influences may also play a role.

Why MRI matters

MRI can show where a meningioma sits and how it relates to nearby structures. Clinical decisions depend on the full scan, symptoms, growth over time, and specialist assessment.

03PITUITARY GLAND

Pituitary tumor

A growth in or around the pituitary gland, which helps regulate hormones. Many pituitary tumors are benign, but they may affect hormone production or press on nearby structures.

What causes it?

The cause of most pituitary tumors is not known. Changes acquired in pituitary cells may contribute; rare inherited conditions, including MEN1 and other genetic syndromes, can increase risk.

Why MRI matters

Dedicated imaging can help examine the pituitary region. Hormone testing, symptoms, and medical evaluation may also be needed to understand a finding.

04DATASET LABEL

No-tumor class

This is the dataset label notumor. It means the model selected the class associated with images labelled as not showing a tumor in its training data.

What does it mean?

It does not prove that a scan is normal or rule out disease. The model can make mistakes and may not generalise to images from other scanners, hospitals, or patient groups.

How to interpret it

Use this output only for project demonstration and learning. Real clinical interpretation belongs to qualified healthcare professionals.

ⓘ

Medical accuracy note: The causes of most brain tumors are not fully understood. These summaries describe general research knowledge, not the cause of any individual person's tumor. References: National Cancer Institute — CNS tumors, NCI — pituitary tumors, and American Cancer Society — causes and risk factors.
